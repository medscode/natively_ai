# Case Study — Gift Deed vs Will (Tax Comparison)

> Source authority: Custom-drafted (illustrative)
> Last updated: 2026-08-11

---

## Facts (anonymized)

- **Client**: Hindu male, age 65, retired. 1 son and 1
  daughter (both married, both with their own children).
- **Assets**:
  - **Mumbai apartment** (self-acquired, 1995, current
    value ₹3 crore).
  - **Bangalore plot** (self-acquired, 2000, current
    value ₹1.5 crore).
  - **Investment portfolio** (₹80 lakh).
  - **EPF, PPF** (₹50 lakh).
- **Question**: Should the client gift the Mumbai apartment
  to the son **now**, or include it in the will for after
  death? Same question for the Bangalore plot (to the
  daughter).
- **Concerns**:
  - Tax efficiency (stamp duty, capital gains, §56(2)(vii)).
  - Future control (can the client reclaim if the son
    becomes irresponsible?).
  - Second-wife risk (current wife is alive; no second
    marriage planned, but who knows).
  - Equality between the two children.

---

## Key legal questions

1. **Gift deed vs will — which is more tax-efficient?**
2. **Stamp duty on gift deed** — same rate as sale in most
   states (5-7% of market value).
3. **Capital gains on gift** — none on the donor (no
   consideration). The donee's cost basis = the donor's cost
   basis (§49 IT Act).
4. **§56(2)(x) IT Act** — stamp duty value of immovable
   property received without consideration is taxable to
   the donee (if not a relative). **Exception**: gifts from
   relatives (parent-to-child = relative, exempt).
5. **Will** — no stamp duty on will. Capital gains
   computed on sale by heir (deceased's cost basis +
   indexation).
6. **Future control** — gift deed is **immediate** and
   **irrevocable** (unless reserved). Will is **ambulatory
   and revocable**.
7. **Reclamation** — can the client reclaim a gifted
   property? Only if the deed expressly reserves a
   reversion, or if the donee agrees to reconvey.

---

## Pattern (how the AI should think about this)

For most clients, the **will** is the **default** estate
planning tool for immovable as well as movable property:
- No immediate stamp duty/mandatory registration
- No capital gains to the donor
- No §56(2)(x) to the donee
- Ambulatory and revocable (client can change the will)
- No loss of control during lifetime

The **gift deed** is preferred when:
- The client wants to **lock in the inheritance** (e.g.,
  to prevent second-marriage claims).
- The client has **no need for the asset** anymore (e.g.,
  moving to a smaller house, or moving abroad).
- The stamp duty + capital gains are **acceptable** trade-
  offs for **certainty of transfer**.
- The client is in **legal conflict** with the heir (e.g.,
  estrangement from a son) and wants to disinherit the heir
  from the gifted property (but a will is better here).

The AI should ask:
- Does the client want **certainty** or **flexibility**?
- Is the client **healthy and active** (gift deed is fine)
  or **elderly and at risk** (will is fine, or consider a
  trust)?
- Is the asset **low-cost-basis and high-value** (gift now
  locks in the heir's tax position; will triggers §45 capital
  gains on sale by heir)?

## Tax comparison

| Factor | Gift deed (now) | Will (after death) |
|---|---|---|
| Stamp duty | Same as sale rate (5-7% of market value) | Nominal (₹50-500) |
| Donor capital gains | None | None |
| Donee income (during life) | §56(2)(x) — stamp duty value taxable (unless relative) | None (inheritance exempt) |
| Donee capital gains (on later sale) | Cost basis = donor's cost basis (indexed from donor's acquisition) | Cost basis = donor's cost basis (indexed from donor's acquisition) |
| Revocability | Generally irrevocable (unless reserved) | Ambulatory and revocable |
| Risk of future challenge | Low (gift is final) | High (will contests common) |
| Creditor protection | Stronger (asset is out of donor's estate) | Weaker (asset is in donor's estate until death) |

## Applicable statutes

- **§122 TPA** — gift of movable or immovable property.
- **§17 RA 1908** — registration of gift deed for
  immovable property.
- **§9 TPA** — oral gift of immovable property is void.
- **§56(2)(x) IT Act** — receipt of immovable property
  without consideration (gift to non-relative).
- **§45 + §48 + §49 IT Act** — capital gains on sale by
  donee.
- **§10(1) IT Act** — inheritance is not taxable income.
- **§30 HSA** — Hindu can will absolute property.

## Behavioral template (for the AI)

When this case pattern matches, the AI should:

1. **Default to "will"** unless the client has a specific
   reason for the gift deed.
2. **Explain the tax trade-offs** clearly.
3. **Flag the future-control issue** — gift deed = lost
   control.
4. **Recommend a trust** for asset protection (often
   better than a gift deed because it preserves some
   control).
5. **Address the §56(2)(x) issue** if the donee is not a
   relative (but parent-to-child is exempt).

## Common pitfalls to flag

1. **"Gift deed avoids stamp duty"** — false. Gift deed
   attracts the same stamp duty as a sale in most states.
2. **"Gifted property doesn't attract capital gains"** —
   true for the donor, but the donee inherits the donor's
   cost basis (§49).
3. **"Will avoids all tax"** — true for inheritance
   itself, but the heir pays capital gains on later sale
   based on the deceased's cost basis.
4. **"Gift deed is final — no challenges"** — generally
   true, but a gift can be challenged for fraud, undue
   influence, or lack of donor's mental capacity at the
   time of the gift.
5. **"Both children must get the same value"** — not
   required. The will / gift can be uneven. But the
   client should be advised of the **legal risk of
   excluding a child** (especially a financially
   dependent one).

## Suggested instrument bundle (depending on client's choice)

### If client chooses gift deed:
1. **Gift deed** for the Mumbai apartment (to son) — drafted
   carefully, registered, stamp duty paid.
2. **Will** — covers Bangalore plot (to daughter), investment
   portfolio, EPF, etc.
3. **Reconciliation** with the daughter — communicate the
   plan, document the rationale, avoid surprise.

### If client chooses will:
1. **Will** — covers all assets, with specified distribution
   to son (Mumbai apartment) and daughter (Bangalore plot).
2. **Trust** for any portion intended for minor grandchildren
   (if applicable).
3. **Annual review** — the will is ambulatory; the client
   should review every 3-5 years or on major life events
   (marriage, divorce, child, etc.).

### Hybrid approach (often best):
1. **Will** — primary instrument.
2. **Gift deed** for one specific high-value asset where
   certainty is important.
3. **Trust** for any portion that needs asset protection
   or controlled distribution.

## When to cite this case

- Client asks: "Should I gift now or leave in the will?"
- Client has high-value, low-cost-basis property.
- Client has concerns about future claims.
- Client wants to differentiate between heirs.
- Client has retirement / long-term-care needs.
- Tax efficiency is a major concern.
- Cross-border elements (NRI, foreign heirs).
